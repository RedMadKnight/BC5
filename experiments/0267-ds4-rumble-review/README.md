# 0267 — The maintainer's DualShock 4 session (F214): the rumble fallback most likely ran on the title's audio thread, the TV sound after the replug was the pad-speaker port on a blocking default-sink device, and the rumble write sat on the title's own threads

*Read-only analysis of the maintainer's play logs of 2026-10-24 16:01–16:14 (copies in `~/bc5-work/play-review-20261024/`) and of the host and SDL code, with exp 0268's measurements of the same session. No run of ours. The fix proposed here is a diff in `raw/`, not applied to main's patch 0001. A first version of this write-up claimed the title opened no vibration port; that rested on the audio trace, which never lists a port without an SDL device (see result 1), and was withdrawn.*

**Question.** The maintainer played with a DualShock 4 (no DualSense): the second title rumbled, then after an unplug and replug the rumble stopped and "the vibration went to the speakers" until the emulator restarted, with hitches not seen before; the lean build hung after a strong hitch with a cutscene-order glitch; ASTRO BOT rumbled the same way. (a) Why does the vibration land on the default sink after the reconnect, and how to fix it? (b) Can the rumble fallback (F214) stall the audio thread and explain the hitches and the order glitch through the audio clock?

**Setup.** Logs: `second-normal-1601.log` (in fact the 16:05 run, normal build `ce2e5d7a` of 15:26, 1,738 lines: the launcher's environment, bc5-direct lines, the fps counter and the audio trace), `second-lean-1611.log` (16:08, lean build; no fps or audio trace in that build), `astro-normal.log` (16:11, 50 lines, no traces). Greps under `nice -n 19 ionice -c3` with no emulator running, before the maintainer's 16:22 session; `raw/play-logs-extract.txt` holds the extracted lines. Code: KytyPlus f266548 + main's patch 0001 (`audio.cpp`, `controller.cpp`, `window.cpp`), SDL2 2.33.0 in the tree (`src/joystick/SDL_joystick.c`, `linux/SDL_sysjoystick.c`, `hidapi/SDL_hidapi_ps4.c`). The launcher sets `SDL_JOYSTICK_DISABLE_UDEV=1`. Exp 0268 (the cutscene session, same logs plus the PipeWire journal) is taken as given where cited.

**Result.**
1. **Which path rumbled the DualShock 4 is not decidable from these logs; the audio fallback most likely ran.** The audio trace of the 16:05 run lists port 0 type 0 (8 ch), port 1 type 0 (8 ch) for its first ~33 s and port 1 as type 4 (pad speaker, 1 ch) from t = 82.9 s, and **no type-10 line**. That absence proves nothing: a vibration port with no SDL device returns from `QueueSdlAudio` right after the fallback, before `tr_calls++`, and the trace skips ports with `tr_calls == 0` (patch 0001 around `QueueSdlAudio`; exp 0268 result 3); the `no controller audio device` LOGF does not print in play mode either. Positive evidence that the title does stream haptics with this pad: the lean session of the same controller logged `bc5 ajm: ATRAC9 vibration layout 7 decoded by vgmstream (haptics)` (exp 0268 `raw/lean-1611-lines.txt:20`), the haptics stream F301 decodes into the vibration port. So with a DualShock 4 the title most likely opened its vibration ports as with a DualSense, found no `DualSense` device, and every block went through `PadRumbleFromVibrationFrames` → `ControllerAudioRumble` → `SDL_GameControllerRumble` on the title's audio thread (F214), with real data since F301. Whether classic `scePadSetVibration` (through `ControllerRumble`, the path F214 added "on the way") contributed as well is unknown; neither path logs anything today. The diff adds the lines that decide it (`rumble:` and, under `KYTY_BC5_INPUT_TRACE=1`, one line per change naming the caller's duration: 100 ms is the fallback, 5,000 ms a classic call). `KYTY_BC5_PAD_RUMBLE_FALLBACK=0` is therefore a valid A/B arm for this title.
2. **"The vibration went to the speakers" is the pad-speaker port, and it is a blocking port.** At t = 82.9 s the title closed port 1 and opened it as a pad-speaker port, 1 ch 48 kHz, 94 pushes/s, and the host put it on the default output because no device named `DualSense` exists (the DualShock 4's own sink is "Sony Interactive Entertainment Wireless Controller", 2 ch 32 kHz, exp 0268; `OpenSdlDevice` matches the name `KYTY_BC5_PAD_AUDIO_DEVICE`, default `DualSense`). The port's effects (impacts and haptic-like sounds that a DualShock 4 plays from its own speaker on a console) therefore came out of the TV from then on. Why only after the replug: the title re-initialised the pad and opened the speaker port then. Because this port has `pad_route == 0`, it is an **ordinary blocking port**: only pad-routed ports are made non-blocking (F72). Exp 0268 result 2 measured it holding the title's audio thread 493 and 635 ms at t 149–150 s, which the main port shows as the push-rate drop to 0.835 and 0.816 with its queue at 0 (`raw/play-logs-extract.txt`): about 0.36 s of main mix missing, an audible hitch, and the same loss on the title's audio-driven clock. Timeline from the fps counter: a 269 ms gap at t = 35 s and a 9.1 s audio silence at 32.7–41.8 s with 12–15 flips/s (the unplug; the title stopped pushing audio, as a console title does when its pad disconnects), 14–15 flips/s during 70–81 s with gaps up to 198 ms (the replug and the re-initialisation), the speaker port at 82.9 s. The other main-port stall seconds (131, 154, 156 at rate 0.19, 163, 165) have low hold figures too: the title's thread was not waiting in the host's queue there, and no pad event sits next to them.
3. **Why the rumble stopped after the replug: not visible in the log, three candidates from the code.** No pad-device lines exist in play mode, so this is from `window.cpp` and `controller.cpp`. (1) `window.cpp` opens a replugged pad as a *raw joystick* when `SDL_GameControllerOpen` fails ("Controller opened as raw joystick") and still connects it to slot 0; `ControllerRumble` only knows `SDL_GameControllerFromInstanceID`, which is null for a raw joystick → no rumble, input intact. With `SDL_JOYSTICK_DISABLE_UDEV=1` SDL finds a replugged pad by inotify on `/dev/input` (evdev) while its hidapi driver re-enumerates every few seconds; which of the two claims the DualShock 4 after a replug decides the GUID and whether a game-controller mapping matches. (2) If the removal event did not reach `GameController::Disconnect`, the new instance binds to slot 1 and slot 0 keeps a dead id: rumble goes to a device that is gone. (3) The DualSense native driver is Windows-only, not involved. The diff covers (1) with a fallback to `SDL_JoystickFromInstanceID` + `SDL_JoystickRumble` and makes (2) visible by logging once per pad instance when the write fails or finds no SDL device.
4. **(b) The rumble write runs on the title's threads and takes SDL's joystick lock.** `SDL_JoystickRumble` (SDL 2.33.0, `SDL_joystick.c:1406`) does `SDL_LockJoysticks()`, then the driver's `Rumble`: on evdev an `ioctl(EVIOCSFF)` plus a `write()` of the play event (`linux/SDL_sysjoystick.c:1714`), on hidapi a report write to the device. The window thread holds the same lock while it opens and probes devices on a hotplug (`MaybeAddDevice`, inotify, the 3-second rescan without udev), work that takes tens to hundreds of milliseconds with a USB device. The callers are the F214 fallback on the title's **audio thread** (one write per change or per 40 ms while non-zero, `controller.cpp` `ControllerAudioRumble`), and `scePadSetVibration` on whichever title thread calls it. A write blocked behind a hotplug scan holds that thread for the scan; on the audio thread that pauses the clock the title paces itself by (F211, F198/F242), the mechanism question (b) names. The trace cannot time it (`held` only counts the queue wait, and the vibration port has no trace line), so this stays a plausible path, not a measured one; the hitches that are measured (result 2) come from the blocking pad-speaker port.
5. **The fix (`raw/rumble-thread-and-speaker-off.diff`, 114 changed lines against main's patched tree), three parts.** (i) The rumble write moves off the calling thread: `ControllerRumble` stores the latest value and signals a worker thread, which does the SDL call; neither the title's audio thread nor its pad thread takes the joystick lock or touches the device, and a value that arrives during a write replaces the pending one (latest wins). This is what makes the fallback's audio-thread call harmless whatever SDL does underneath. (ii) The pad-speaker port on the default sink never blocks (new `PortOut::pad_nb`, set when a type-4 port opens with no controller device): a queue over eight buffers is cleared, never waited for, like the pad-routed ports (F72). That is exp 0268's 1.1 s hold removed at its source; `KYTY_BC5_PAD_SPEAKER=main-blocking` keeps the old blocking port for an A/B. (iii) `KYTY_BC5_PAD_SPEAKER=off` keeps such a port silent instead of playing it on the TV; `main` and unset both mean the non-blocking default-sink port. Plus the diagnostics of result 1 and 3. Syntax-checked with clang-cl against the writing machine's older tree: the errors there are symbols other parts of main's patch define (`BC5_DIAG_ENV`, `Log::Bc5LoggingOn`, `setenv`, `KernelClocksPaused`, and `ControllerAudioRumble` from that tree's reverted header), none in the changed regions; not built.
6. **Lean and ASTRO.** The lean build's hang (16:08–16:11) left no audio or pad trace (exp 0268 result 1); the cutscene-order glitch is not explained by anything here. ASTRO BOT's 50-line log has no trace; "rumble behaves the same" there is consistent with the fallback running, as in the second title.

**Verdict.** (a) After the replug the second title opened its pad-speaker port, and the host plays that on the default output when the pad has no audio device (F69's design), as a blocking port that held the audio thread for 1.1 s in two seconds of the 16:05 session (exp 0268). The diff makes it non-blocking by default and switchable off. The lost rumble is most likely the replugged pad coming back as a raw joystick or on another slot, which the diff handles and logs. (b) The F214 fallback very likely ran on the title's audio thread with real haptics data; whether its SDL write stalled that thread is not measurable from these logs, so `KYTY_BC5_PAD_RUMBLE_FALLBACK=0` is a valid A/B arm, and the diff takes the write off that thread regardless. The measured hitch of the session is the blocking pad-speaker port, not the rumble. Verification: one DualShock 4 session on a build with the diff, `KYTY_BC5_INPUT_TRACE=1`, an unplug and replug, the `rumble:` and `input trace: rumble` lines (which also say which path drives the pad), and the audio trace's hold on port 1 staying at 0; then the A/B arms `KYTY_BC5_PAD_RUMBLE_FALLBACK=0` and `KYTY_BC5_PAD_SPEAKER=main-blocking` if the hitches persist.
